Splitpot privacy policy

Last updated 6 September 2026.

Splitpot is an Android app for keeping score at home poker games: who bought in for what, who cashed out, and who owes whom at the end of the night. This policy describes exactly what the app stores, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

What is collected

WhatWhy
Your email address It is your login. Sign-in is by a one-time emailed code or Google sign-in; Splitpot never stores a password.
Your display name, and optionally a phone number Shown on your own games. The phone number is optional and never shown to players.
Names of the people you play with, and optionally their payment details You type these in. They let the app say who owes whom, and show a payee's details on a shared ledger when they are owed money.
Game records: buy-ins, cash-outs, blinds, dates, locations you enter This is the app's whole purpose.
An advertising identifier, used by Google AdMob The app shows one banner advert. See "Advertising" below.

You are entering other people's information. The names, and any payment details you record, belong to your friends rather than to you. Only enter what they are happy for you to keep, and tell them before you share a link that shows their results.

What is not collected

  • No contacts. The app never reads your address book.
  • No location. "Location" on a game is a free-text label you type, such as "John's place".
  • No SMS, no call logs, no photos, no files.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting product is built into the app.
  • No passwords, because there are none.

Where it is stored

Data is held in a Supabase project hosted on Amazon Web Services in the Mumbai region (ap-south-1), in India. It is protected by row-level security, which means each host can only ever read and write their own rows. The anonymous role used by shared web pages has no direct access to any table at all.

Some data is also kept on your phone so the app works with no signal: the game you last opened, your player list, your profile, and any buy-ins or cash-outs waiting to be sent. That copy is private to the app and is removed when you sign out or delete your account.

Links you choose to share

Two kinds of link can be shared, and both are your decision:

  • A ledger link for one game. Anyone holding it can see that game's results, and a payee's payment details when they are owed money.
  • A standings link covering every finished game. This is off by default. Turning it on publishes every player's name and how much they are up or down to anyone with the link. Turning it off makes the link dead immediately.

Neither link is listed anywhere or indexed by search engines, but a link is a link: anyone it is forwarded to can open it.

Advertising

Splitpot shows a single banner advert served by Google AdMob. AdMob may use an advertising identifier to select adverts and measure them. You can reset or delete that identifier in Android under Settings, then Privacy, then Ads. Google's handling of this data is described in the Google privacy policy. Nothing you type into Splitpot — no names, amounts or results — is ever sent to AdMob.

Who else sees it

Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared for advertising. Data reaches only:

  • Supabase and Amazon Web Services, which host the database and the sign-in service.
  • Google AdMob, for the advert, as described above.
  • Anyone you deliberately send a ledger or standings link to.

Deleting everything

In the app, open Settings, scroll to Account, and choose Delete my account. That removes every game, every buy-in and cash-out, your whole player list, your profile and your login, in one step. It cannot be undone, no backup copy is kept, and any links you shared stop working at once.
